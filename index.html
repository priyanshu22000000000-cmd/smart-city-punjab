What the mission was

The Smart Cities Mission picked 100 Indian cities. Each set up a company, a Special Purpose Vehicle, to rebuild one model zone and add city-wide technology like integrated command and control centres.

  1. 25 June 2015Mission launched for 100 cities.
  2. January 2016Ludhiana selected in the first round.
  3. September 2016Jalandhar and Amritsar selected; Amritsar was proposed by the state in place of Mohali.
  4. 2020 to 2024Planned as a five-year programme, the deadline was extended three times.
  5. 31 March 2025Mission formally closed. Only 18 of the 100 cities had finished every project.
  6. 31 March 2026Nationally, 7,790 of 8,064 projects (97%) complete.

The environmental lens

Smart city projects are judged by what they do for water, waste and air, not just by how many are finished.

20 lakh t

Wariana dump, Jalandhar

An estimated 20 lakh tonnes of old waste, about 50 metres high. The Punjab Pollution Control Board flagged groundwater risks before the National Green Tribunal, which ordered a fresh inspection in August 2026. In one six-month period, 75,000 tonnes were cleared while about 50,000 tonnes of fresh waste arrived.

300%

Amritsar’s groundwater

In Amritsar’s urban block, people pull out more than three times the groundwater that rain puts back. That’s why the canal-water plant at Vallah matters.

2024 > 2022

Buddha Nullah and the Sutlej

The Central Pollution Control Board found the water worse in 2024 than in 2022, despite a rejuvenation project worth about ₹840 crore.

AQI 294

Jalandhar’s air

Dug-up roads for unfinished projects added dust, and the city’s air quality index touched 294, which is classed as very poor.

Problems and what comes next

Delays are the common thread. In a 2024 budget debate, Rajya Sabha member Vikramjit Singh Sahney said only 5 of 72 work orders in Punjab’s smart cities had been completed. Leaders across parties have sought inquiries into alleged irregularities in Jalandhar, and the Centre sought a report. Allegations are not findings until proven, but residents still report potholes, garbage and broken streetlights.

Now that the mission has closed, the Housing Ministry has asked states to finish pending works, plan long-term maintenance, repurpose the Special Purpose Vehicles and keep command centres running.

The next big source of money is the Urban Challenge Fund: ₹1 lakh crore, approved by the Union Cabinet in February 2026. It covers 25% of a project’s cost if the city raises at least half from the market. The future favours cities that can plan, finance and maintain, not just build.

A smart city starts with you

Sustainable Development Goal 11 calls for sustainable cities. These are things anyone can do today.

  • Separate wet and dry waste at home, so less ends up on dumps like Wariana.
  • Save water: fix leaks, turn off running taps, and harvest rainwater where you can.
  • Report broken streetlights, potholes and garbage to your municipal corporation.
  • Ask where public money went. Projects improve when citizens follow up.
